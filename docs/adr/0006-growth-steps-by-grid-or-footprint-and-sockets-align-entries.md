# Level 1 growth steps by the snap grid or footprint; drawn sockets align entry arrows or back edges

A design without socket arrows grows through implicit sockets on its footprint edges: the next copy is the same design, facing the same way, one repeat further along each chosen direction. Only copies of the project's own design open implicit sockets: a plain design that a drawn socket names is an end piece and opens nothing. The repeat is the blueprint's snap grid when it has one, else its footprint, so a plain blueprint tiles with no markers at all. A design with socket arrows grows only through them, and the direction choice in the window does not apply.

On a drawn socket, the child is turned so that its entry arrow faces the socket's direction, and placed so that the entry lands on the socket. A child design without an entry arrow attaches by the middle of its back edge, unturned: that edge's row sits on the socket's tile, centred along it. This is the entry rule with an implied entry, so one rule serves both kinds of child. A socket goes on the first tile outside its design's edge and an entry on the design's own edge tile, and either way the copies fit flush. A child that would overlap the stamp it grows from closes as "overlaps-parent" and is marked on the map. On a design whose entities snap to the 2x2 rail grid, a socket that cannot put the child's first row on its tile closes as "off-grid" instead of leaving a gap or an overlap.

Remote stamps and project seeds keep DESIGN.md section 4's coordinate rule: a stamp lands by its entry arrow, or, without one, by the vanilla anchor, its footprint centred on the point. A review spike built the player's own blueprint at tile-centre points and matched this for 3x3, 2x2, 6x3 and 5x3 designs.

An earlier version of this decision put an entry-less child's vanilla anchor on the socket (D18 replaced it). Every such socket closed as off-grid, because sockets sit on tile centres and build positions need whole tiles. Centring the child on the socket instead would have made a socket just outside the edge overlap its own parent.

Supporting choices:

- **Seeds take one of four places.** `position` lands the entry arrow on that tile (the vanilla anchor without one), as a remote stamp does; `build_position` is the raw build position and must sit on the design's lattice; `point` centres the first copy there (a click); `area` fills from its top-left corner (a drag).
- **Absolute snapping is honoured.** A blueprint with absolute snapping keeps its world grid: a click seeds the copy in the grid cell under it (the test compares against vanilla `build_blueprint` in all four directions; spike S1, Q1 f), and a drag starts at the first placement on the grid whose footprint lies inside the rectangle, wherever the content sits in its cell. Implicit children step one grid from their parent's footprint, so copies of an on-grid seed stay on the grid and a seed placed off it by a raw `build_position` keeps growing flush with itself. Remote `position` and `build_position` stay exact. There is no Spacing field (the snap grid already gives gaps) and no Shift field: a staggered layout is drawn with socket arrows.
- **A drag fills from its top-left corner.** It seeds there and spreads east and south, and the rectangle bounds the growth. A click seeds a copy centred on the clicked tile and uses the window's directions.
- **Growth is breadth-first.** Depth-first and nearest-first are later options.
- **Sockets open when their instance completes.** Growth runs exactly as fast as the robots, and an instance that ends incomplete opens nothing.
